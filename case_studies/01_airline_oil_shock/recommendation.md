# Recommendation

## Recommended strategy

Management should avoid responding to an oil shock with one single lever.

A balanced response would combine:

1. **Selective fare increases** on routes with stronger pricing power.
2. **Higher hedging discipline** to reduce exposure to extreme fuel volatility.
3. **Capacity reduction on structurally weak routes** rather than across-the-board cuts.
4. **Protection of premium and high-margin routes** where demand is more resilient.
5. **Expansion of ancillary revenue** to reduce dependence on base fares.
6. **Operational cost actions** that improve economics without weakening customer experience.

---

## Why this approach

The central trade-off is between protecting near-term margins and preserving long-term network strength.

Aggressive fare increases can hurt demand.

Aggressive capacity cuts can weaken market position.

Doing nothing can allow the fuel shock to destroy profitability.

The strongest response therefore combines pricing, hedging, route economics, and cost actions.

---

## Key indicators to monitor

- Oil and jet fuel prices
- Fuel-cost share of operating expense
- Fare elasticity
- Load factor
- Revenue per available seat mile
- Cost per available seat mile
- Premium demand
- Competitor fare actions
- FX rates
- Hedging coverage
